formatDateTime generator

Dates in Power Automate arrive as UTC timestamps like 2026-08-14T13:05:00Z, which nobody wants to read in an email. Pick where the date comes from and how it should look, optionally convert it out of UTC with convertTimeZone, and copy the finished expression.

Date Formatter

Pick a source and a format to get the formatDateTime expression.


      
      
    

  






  

    
How to use it

    
    
      
  1. Choose the date source, or enter your own expression.
    2. 
      
  2. Choose a format, or type a custom format string.
    3. 
      
  3. To convert out of UTC, enter a Windows time zone name such as GMT Standard Time.
    4. 
      
  4. Copy the expression into the Expression tab of the dynamic content panel.
    5. 
    

    
Nothing you type here is sent anywhere. The tool runs entirely in your browser.

  






  

    
Format strings, with examples

    
Power Automate uses .NET date format strings. Each output below is for Friday 14 August 2026 at 13:05 UTC.

    

      
Format stringOutput

      
        
dd/MM/yyyy14/08/2026

        
MM/dd/yyyy08/14/2026

        
yyyy-MM-dd2026-08-14

        
d MMMM yyyy14 August 2026

        
dddd, d MMMM yyyyFriday, 14 August 2026

        
ddd d MMMFri 14 Aug

        
MMMM yyyyAugust 2026

        
HH:mm13:05

        
hh:mm tt01:05 PM

        
h:mm tt1:05 PM

        
dd/MM/yyyy HH:mm14/08/2026 13:05

      
    

    
Letter case matters. MM is the month and mm is minutes, so dd/mm/yyyy gives 14/05/2026. HH is the 24-hour clock and hh the 12-hour one.


    
Related expressions

    

      
ExpressionWhat it does

      
        
utcNow('dd/MM/yyyy')Formats the current time directly, without formatDateTime

        
formatDateTime(utcNow(), 'dddd d MMMM', 'fr-FR')Writes day and month names in another language, here vendredi 14 août

        
parseDateTime('14/08/2026', 'en-GB', 'dd/MM/yyyy')Turns text such as a date typed into a form into a timestamp you can format or compare

      
    

    
All of these work in UTC. For UK or other local time, set a time zone in the generator above, which wraps the date in convertTimeZone.
